<Phase num="01" title="The Problem" />

People weren't dropping out because they didn't care. The process asked too much, too fast, and every extra decision gave them a reason to stop.

As a marketer, I know that moment well: the second someone hesitates, you've lost them. The job was to find out where that happened, and why.

<PullQuote size="md" source="How might we">
  How might we reduce cognitive load during complex onboarding so users feel guided rather than
  interrogated?
</PullQuote>

<Phase num="02" title="My Role & Process" />

My focus was the human side: mapping user journeys and turning qualitative feedback into decisions. I laid the whole journey out in Miro, marked where people slowed down, and asked of every step whether it had earned its place.

<PullQuote source="The insight behind Plan A">
  Users don't fail because they lack interest; they fail because cognitive load creates hesitation.
</PullQuote>

Each round of testing gave us another step to remove or simplify. Huyen and I checked every change against what users said, not what we assumed.

<Phase num="03" title="The Solution" />

A guided journey with fewer steps and one clear decision at a time. The information architecture does the heavy lifting, so users don't have to.
