<Phase num="01" title="The Problem" />

Designing for people who are already busy is its own challenge. They don't have spare attention for another app, and they'll drop anything that asks for it.

So we left the screen and went to where the behavior actually happens: the physical spaces and routines users move through every day.

<PullQuote size="md" source="How might we">
  How might we design an interface that seamlessly integrates into a user's physical routine without
  demanding their undivided attention?
</PullQuote>

<Phase num="02" title="Process" />

I worked as strategist and wireframer. We turned what we saw in real environments into interactions, then wireframed them in Figma so the team had something concrete to react to.

<PullQuote source="The rule we designed by">
  If the value proposition isn't clear within 3 seconds, UI polish won't save the experience.
</PullQuote>

Huyen and I traded wireframes and critiques until each nudge earned its place on the screen.

<Phase num="03" title="Solution" />

A digital tool that nudges without overwhelming: small, clear interactions that fit into a routine instead of interrupting it.
