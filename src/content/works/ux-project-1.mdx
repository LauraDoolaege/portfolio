## Overview

**In short:** young travellers don't pick a city, they follow the one friend who suggests a trip, and most of those plans die in the group chat. We designed a quick Antwerp-themed game the initiator shares with their friends. Playing it collects everyone's dates, and the highest score wins a free drink at a local Antwerp bar.

## The problem

Visit Antwerp struggles to reach **young urban travellers**, and wants them to stay longer than a day trip. Classic tourism campaigns don't work on this group: **they trust friends and creators, not official channels.**

Our research showed the deeper problem. **Young people barely care where they go; it's about the vibe and being together.** Antwerp ticks every box, but so do many other cities. And when someone does suggest a trip, **the plan often stalls because nobody else takes the next step.**

<PullQuote size="md">
  How might we help <mark class="hl">Antwerp's</mark> local flavours and 24/7 pulse stand out naturally for the <mark class="hl">Gen Z
  initiative-taker</mark>, so exploring the city becomes a <mark class="hl">shared experience</mark>, from interest to action?
</PullQuote>

Desk research backed this up: **groups pick what keeps them together, not the “best” option**, and **initiators feel under-appreciated and burned out** from always texting first. We started with Gen Z (18 to 24). After the pitch, the client asked us to include millennials, so the personas span 18 to 35.

## Scope & constraints

- **The brief.** A digital-first concept from Visit Antwerp that feels authentic, not like a classic campaign. It had to combine at least two of: a digital app, a physical activation, a social media format.
- **A city theme.** Interviewees picked Flavours and 24/7 Pulse most, so we built on those. No landmark-showcasing: the client wanted experiences, niche interests and subcultures.
- **A tight timeline.** Concept pitch on 22 May, five weekly sprints, final exhibition in Kortrijk on 24 June.
- **Research abroad, in a day.** Field research happened in Rotterdam, with about 15 interviewees aged 18 to 21.
- **Two light testing rounds.** A wireframe form (4 initiators, 4 followers), then a clickable Figma prototype with 5 testers. Neither tested a real friend group planning a real trip.

## Thought process

The turning point was realising **we didn't need to sell Antwerp at all.** We needed to **help the one friend who starts plans**, and make Antwerp the city where those plans finally happen.

## The solution

**Plan A** is a web app that turns planning a trip into a quick game between friends. Antwerp becomes the city where plans finally make it out of the group chat.

### How it works

<Timeline>
  <TimelineStep title="The hook">
    A micro-influencer or paid creator shares a POV video about always being the one who plans, with
    a link to the game. Testers trusted micro-influencers more than big ones.
  </TimelineStep>
  <TimelineStep title="Onboarding">
    Four short screens explain the idea: compete to win, you're the main character, plan with ease.
    Skippable, with a start screen that sums up the concept.
  </TimelineStep>
  <TimelineStep title="Your character">
    Snap a photo to put your face on the in-game character, or continue with a default one.
  </TimelineStep>
  <TimelineStep title="The game">
    A one-tap runner through Antwerp scenes. Three tries; your best score is the one to beat.
  </TimelineStep>
  <TimelineStep title="The planning">
    One question per screen: nickname, possible dates, players (2 to 10), budget, the group's vibe,
    a deadline for friends to respond, and email. A summary screen lets you check everything.
  </TimelineStep>
  <TimelineStep title="Share and join">
    One tap sends it to Instagram, TikTok or any chat. Friends vote on the dates, play the game and
    see the leaderboard.
  </TimelineStep>
  <TimelineStep title="The plan lands">
    Everyone gets an email with the leaderboard, the date and a local bar matched to the group's
    vibe. The winner gets a QR coupon for a free drink, plus small Antwerp detours nearby.
  </TimelineStep>
</Timeline>

## Testing and iteration

The concept changed twice before it got its final shape: **once after the pitch**, through a structured critique, and **again after testing the prototype.**

### Six Thinking Hats: stress-testing the concept

After the pitch we wrote the concept out in full and looked at it through each hat in turn. The Black Hat questions turned into the rules of the second iteration.

<CaseImage
  label="Black Hat: what could go wrong"
  src={blackHat}
  alt="Board of the Black Hat session: risks in the concept and the changes they led to"
  size="wide"
  ratio="4756 / 3592"
  caption="The Black Hat session: the risks we found in the concept and what we changed."
/>

1. **Only one try.** Players might mess up their first run. Now: unlimited practice rounds before the real game.
2. **Fewer friends play than planned.** The plan would never be sent. Now: the initiator sets a response window, and the plan goes out when everyone has played or the timer runs out.
3. **The winner can't make the chosen date.** Now: the final date is the one most players picked, and if the winner didn't pick it, the coupon goes to the highest scorer who did.
4. **Big groups could split up to collect more free drinks.** Now: the coupon links to a random bar matching the group's vibe, and only one person wins per group.

The Red Hat added a gut feeling: **something Antwerp-specific was missing beyond a free drink.** That led to **story-driven collectibles** in the game, like a beer for Antwerp's bar culture or a camera for capturing moments. The concept went from a fun idea to **one that holds up with real friend groups, real bars and real schedules.**

## What we tried and dropped

We cut anything that made the concept heavier, more promotional, or less of a game.

<Disclosure label="See what we dropped">

- **Designing the whole trip experience inside Antwerp.** Our first consult showed we could focus on one moment. We chose the planning phase, where plans die.
- **Turning the game into a form or a “who of us” quiz.** It would break the simple, competitive flow the client liked, so we kept it a real game.
- **Gen Z only.** The client pointed out the problem is just as real for millennials, so we widened the personas to 18 to 35.
- **Hashtag campaigns and highway billboards.** Hashtags feel overdone, and many Gen Z travellers don't drive. Campus and bus-stop posters reach them where they are.

</Disclosure>

## What I'd do differently

**A fun hook only works if people understand what it's for.** Every tester found the concept more fun than planning in the chat, but **not everyone found it more practical.**

- **Test the order, don't debate it.** Initiators felt the game took the focus, and tutors later asked for planning first. Test two versions side by side, early.
- **Make the reward impossible to miss.** Followers didn't notice they could win anything, and some thought any good score earned a drink. Put the rules on the first screen.
- **Test real behaviour, with a wider circle.** A wireframe survey and a prototype don't show whether a friend group commits. A live pilot with real group chats, beyond students aged 18 to 21, would have told us more.
