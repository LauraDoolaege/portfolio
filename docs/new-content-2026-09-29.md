## How I detangle (process)

Label: [ 02 ] The process
Title: How I detangle

01 Sparks
The brief is barely out and tiny ideas are already popping up. I write them all down, even the weird ones.

02 Mapping
Little mind maps everywhere. Everything connects to everything.

03 The knot
And then it gets messy. Too many ideas, too many angles. That's fine. That's where it gets fun.

04 Detangling
I gather as much info as I can about the brand, the people, and the market. The base keeps getting bigger.

05 Looking away
The answer rarely comes from staring at a screen. It comes during a long drive home, a walk with my dog, a game of pool I'm probably losing, or a venting session at the dinner table. Saying things out loud to a sparring partner is often where the knot starts to loosen.

06 The string
Somewhere in there is one loose string. Pull it and the whole knot comes undone.

07 Then I build
First my assumption, then the creative direction, then the technical part. In that order.

## About page

Title: My story didn't start at Devine.

Before I ever opened Figma, I got a bachelor's in marketing, specialising in marketing communication, and graduated cum laude. During my internship at CAW Oost-Vlaanderen I planned and created social media content, wrote the letters sent to policy makers across Belgium, and led a workshop on my own to rethink the content structure of their new website. Looking back, that was my first UX project. I just didn't know it yet.

But honestly, it started way earlier. On the playground I was the kid inventing storylines where everyone got a part. At home I wrote little stories starring my sister's favourite Nintendo characters, just to get her excited about reading. It worked a little too well. She now reads more books than I do ;)

Now I'm in my third year of Digital Design and Development at Devine in Kortrijk, majoring in UX design. Same love for stories, just with more tools.

### In a team

In a team, I'm usually the one holding the thread.
In the first week I get everyone talking and getting to know each other. I love leading brainstorms and hearing people out. But when we get carried away, I'm also the one asking "wait, does this still match what our research told us?" And when it's time to pitch, I structure the story and present it.
